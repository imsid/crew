# Ampere — Acquisition & Marketing Strategy

> Written by the CMO. The Growth Crew mostly *retains and expands* the installed base, but the
> self-serve nudges it drafts must sound like Ampere's marketing voice and route into the same PLG
> motion described here.

## Motion: product-led, sales-assisted at the top

Ampere is **PLG-first**. Developers land on `free`, feel value in minutes, and consumption grows on its
own. Marketing's job is to fill the top of that funnel and to remove friction at each rung of the
consumption ladder (see [[product-and-roadmap]]). Sales is invited in only where the account ceiling and
timing justify a human — never as the default.

### The funnel
```
awareness → free signup → activation (first workflow/artifact) → paid (team) → expansion (business/enterprise)
   CMO owns ───────────────────────────►│◄─── Growth Crew owns retention + expansion ───►
```

## Paid acquisition channels

Ranked by efficiency for our ICP (developers and eng leaders at funded startups):

1. **Developer search / intent** — Google + Bing on high-intent terms ("AI code review", "automated
   refactor", "CI coding agent"). Highest converting; scales with budget.
2. **Sponsorships in dev media** — newsletters (TLDR, Pragmatic Engineer), podcasts, YouTube devrel.
   Brand + mid-funnel.
3. **Social (X / LinkedIn) retargeting** — retarget site + free-tier signups who haven't activated
   `workflow`. Ties directly to the activation gap the Growth Crew also works.
4. **Conference + community** — booths and workshops at eng conferences; open-source sponsorships.
   Enterprise pipeline top-of-funnel.

Guardrail: paid is measured on **activated free signups**, not raw signups — a signup that never runs a
workflow or publishes an artifact is not yet a customer.

## ICP (ideal customer profile)

- **Company:** software companies, 10–2,000 engineers, cloud-native, shipping frequently.
- **Funding:** seed through late-stage; freshly-funded startups are the highest-velocity segment
  (budget + hiring + urgency).
- **Buyer/user:** individual developers (bottoms-up) and eng leaders/platform teams (who consolidate
  spend and sign the `business`/`enterprise` deal).
- **Tech stack fit:** teams already on GitHub/GitLab with CI; the `command`→`workflow` path lands fastest.

## Campaign themes (voice the self-serve nudges should echo)

- **"Ship the boring work to Ampere."** — automation / workflow adoption.
- **"Your codebase, on tap."** — repo-context, always-available help.
- **"Review every PR without hiring for it."** — CI-native review, command→workflow.
- **"Scale spend with controls, not surprises."** — business-tier upgrade (budgets/analytics).

## Self-serve nudge principles (for the Growth Crew's `curate-plays` step)

When a workflow decides the right motion is **self-serve** (hobbyist signals, small startup ceilings,
low-revenue dips), the drafted copy must:

- Be **short, developer-voiced, no fluff.** Devs delete marketing emails; they read a useful tip.
- **Lead with the account's own usage evidence** ("your team ran 40 workflows last week") — Ampere data,
  not a generic pitch.
- **Point at exactly one next rung** (turn on workflow templates; try CI-native review) with a
  one-click path.
- **Never invent numbers.** Only cite what the warehouse actually shows.
- Match a **campaign theme** above so the touch is consistent with what marketing is already saying.

Anything requiring a human (large ceiling, enterprise, high-revenue dip) is handed to Sales — see the
CRO's [[revenue-strategy]] for how that briefing is shaped and routed.
