# Ampere — Personas & Tiers

> The single most important judgment the Growth Crew makes: **the same warehouse row means different
> things depending on who the account is.** 3→9 active devs is a ceiling for one company and a beachhead
> for another. This doc defines the three personas so the agent steps can tell them apart.

## The three customer types

### Hobbyist / individual (usually `free`, occasionally `team`)
- **Shape:** 1–3 developers, one org, personal or side-project domains (gmail, personal sites).
- **Company signal:** none to speak of — no funding, no headcount, tiny or absent domain.
- **What "growth" means:** marginal. A hobbyist doubling from 1→2 devs is not an expansion motion; it's
  noise. Ceiling is low by definition.
- **What a "dip" means:** usually genuine abandonment (finished the project, lost interest). Low
  revenue-weight; **not worth a human play** — at most an automated self-serve nudge.
- **Right motion:** self-serve only. Never route to a human. Cheapest possible touch.

### Startup (`team` → `business`)
- **Shape:** 5–50 developers, a real company domain, seed–Series B funding, actively hiring engineers.
- **Company signal:** meaningful — headcount and funding tell you the ceiling. A funded 30-person startup
  that just raised has room to 3–5× its Ampere footprint.
- **What "growth" means:** real and fast. Crossing into `workflow`, adding devs, climbing tiers. This is
  the sweet spot for **product-led expansion** — a nudge or a light-touch briefing converts.
- **What a "dip" means:** could be a real problem (a champion left, a competitor bake-off, budget freeze).
  Medium-to-high revenue-weight. **Worth a human play** if revenue is material.
- **Right motion:** self-serve nudge for small signals; sales-assisted briefing when the ceiling and
  timing justify a human.

### Enterprise (`business` → `enterprise`)
- **Shape:** 500+ employees, established domain, late-stage or public, many potential teams.
- **Company signal:** large ceiling almost by definition. Nine active developers inside a 4,000-person
  company is a **beachhead**, not a ceiling — the expansion is landing the next team, then the next.
- **What "growth" means:** land-and-expand. A modest usage uptick can justify a **human sales motion this
  week**, because the committed-use contract downstream is large.
- **What a "dip" means:** high stakes even if small in %. A dip in an enterprise account is a
  retention fire — highest revenue-weight, always a human play, often escalated to the CSM.
- **Right motion:** human-led. Sales briefing with usage evidence + company context attached.

## The core contrast (the pitch's example)

> Same org, same warehouse row: **3→9 active devs, tokens +140%**.
>
> - In a **12-person startup**, nine devs is most of the company — that's approaching a ceiling.
>   Nudge to self-serve upgrade; a human motion would be over-investment.
> - In a **4,000-person enterprise** that just raised, nine devs is a **beachhead** worth a human
>   motion *this week* — the ceiling is enormous and the timing (fresh funding) is a moment.
>
> **Code fetches the signal; the agent decides what it means.** That decision requires this persona
> context fused with company enrichment. It cannot be a fixed threshold.

## Tier ↔ persona mapping (typical, not a rule)

| Persona | Typical tiers | Company enrichment expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Hobbyist | free (rarely team) | headcount < 5, no funding, personal domain |
| Startup | team, business | headcount 5–200, seed–Series C, hiring engineers |
| Enterprise | business, enterprise | headcount 500+, late-stage/public, multiple teams |

The workflows never treat tier alone as identity — an enterprise can be on `business` today (that's the
expansion opportunity). Always fuse **usage trajectory × company headroom × timing**. The thresholds and
the specific plays are the CRO's job: see [[revenue-strategy]].
